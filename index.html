Product Overview

“Meet FAMIL.AI.”

FAMIL.AI is an intergenerational AI Literacy and Media & Information Literacy learning game where a parent and child experience the same information scenario from different roles, anchored by our core S.P.O.T. Framework.

“Most digital literacy tools teach people how to check information. FAMIL.AI teaches people to examine why they trusted it in the first place — together.”

Core Evaluation Engine

The S.P.O.T. Framework Explained

FAMIL.AI replaces vague skepticism with a structured 4-pillar evaluation model that players use during investigator rounds and AI debriefs:

S

Source & Timestamp

Investigating original provenance, publication metadata, domain history, and whether the author's credentials match the claim.

P

Persuasion Cues

Identifying emotional triggers, urgency amplifiers, and visual manipulation designed to bypass critical thinking before verification.

O

Objective & Motive

Questioning the underlying intent—whether content is designed to inform, monetize, provoke outrage, or influence public perception.

T

Triangulation

Cross-referencing claims across independent corroborating sources rather than relying on a single emotional social media feed post.

Step 3 / Role Reversal

Role Reversal Exchange

After the first round, parents and children switch roles. The former content composer experiences why the investigator questioned them, and vice versa. This breaks generational barriers and builds critical reasoning from both angles.

Round 1

Child = Composer | Parent = Investigator

Round 2 (Reversed)

Child = Investigator | Parent = Composer

Bridging generational divides and building mutual empathy
Step 4

S.P.O.T. AI Debrief

AI analyzes the differences between the family's dual decisions:

S
Source

AI points out unverified source provenance.

P
Persuasion Cues

AI dissects exaggerated sensational headlines.

O
Objective

Why this content exists (clickbait, monetization, panic induction).

T
Triangulation

Recommending reputable independent sources for cross-checking.

Step 5 / Conclusion

Continuing the Family Dialogue

The game concludes with guided discussion prompts, helping families compare parent and child choices and turning any disagreements into practical learning moments.

Evidence & Testing

“Strengthening Validation and Impact.”

A rigorous distinction between existing external evidence, our core hypotheses, and planned pilot testing.

What We Know

Existing External Research Findings

  • Adolescent exposure to unverified online misinformation (~2/3 in Norway study).
  • Confidence vs actual ability gap documented by Ofcom media literacy research.
What We Hypothesize

Core Research Proposition

We hypothesize that structured intergenerational dialogue can improve not only recognition of misinformation cues, but also the ability to explain why information feels convincing.

What We Will Test [TO BE VALIDATED]

Planned Pilot Measurement

Planned pilot study across a cohort of 50 parents and children aged 10–18, measuring behavioral pause times, follow-up conversation frequency, and persuasive literacy explanation depth.

Success Metrics

“Success is behavior, not downloads.”

Evaluating genuine impact through behavioral indicators and longitudinal family habits.

Indicator 01

Behavioral Pause

Time-to-react and pause-before-sharing behavior during simulated scenarios.

Pilot hypothesis: participants will show a measurable increase in deliberate pause behavior after completing the intervention.
Indicator 02

Conversational Frequency

7-day and 30-day follow-up surveys assessing whether families continue discussing digital information after the game.

Key outcome: whether the intervention creates a habit of family dialogue beyond the game session.
Indicator 03

Persuasive Literacy

S.P.O.T. signal-recognition and explanation tasks.

Key outcome: whether participants can explain not only whether content is trustworthy, but why it initially felt convincing.
